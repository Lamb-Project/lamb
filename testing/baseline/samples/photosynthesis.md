# Photosynthesis — Course Notes, Week 3

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose.
It occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells.

## The two stages

**Light-dependent reactions** take place in the thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll
absorbs photons, water is split, oxygen is released as a by-product, and the
energy carriers ATP and NADPH are produced.

**The Calvin cycle** takes place in the stroma. Carbon dioxide is fixed by the
enzyme RuBisCO and, using the ATP and NADPH from the first stage, reduced into
glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, from which glucose is assembled.

## Overall equation

Six molecules of carbon dioxide plus six of water, driven by light, yield one
molecule of glucose and six of oxygen.

## Exam note

Students frequently confuse the two stages. The light-dependent reactions need
light directly; the Calvin cycle does not, though it depends on the products of
the first stage, which is why calling it the "dark reaction" is misleading.
